How a fight is decided

Every card in the game is built from three public numbers — subscribers, total views, video count — plus the channel's age. This is the whole path from those numbers to a winner, with real cards from the live 15,831-card deck as the worked examples.

Size buys a budget. / Shape decides the fight.
Step one

From a channel to a combatant

Nothing about a card is authored. The chain below runs the same way for every channel, in the browser, every time — which is why two players on two devices always compute an identical fight.

Input 3 numbers + age subscribers, views, videos, and when the channel started.
Six axes What it's like influence, maturity, punch, devotion, cadence, velocity — each 0–100.
Budget How many points 200 + 25 × influence. The largest channel alive gets ~1.32× the smallest.
Shape Where they go the five axes as fractions of each other, summing to 1. Size cancels out.
Output Five stats + class budget × share, per stat. The class is a read of the shape.
Why the budget is nearly flat

A 300M-subscriber channel receives about 1.3× the points of a 1,000-subscriber one — not 10×. If size bought both more health and more damage, no small card could ever win and rarity would decide fights. Instead size buys a slightly bigger pot, and the channel's own proportions decide what that pot is spent on.

AxisMeasuresPays forDe-sized?
influence Subscriber count The size of the budget only — it is the size term
maturity How long the channel has existed HP Not de-sized — age genuinely differs
punch Views per upload ATK Yes — residual vs. its own size
devotion Views per subscriber DEF Yes — residual vs. its own size
cadence Uploads per year SPD Yes — residual vs. its own size
velocity Subscribers won per year MOM Yes — residual vs. its own size

"Residual" is the load-bearing word. An axis doesn't score raw views-per-video; it scores the gap between this channel's views-per-video and what a channel of its size normally manages. 50 means exactly on trend. Above 50 means punching above its weight. That is why a 200K-subscriber channel whose videos land like a 5M one becomes genuinely dangerous, and why a giant coasting on reach does not.

Step two

What the five stats actually do

StatIn the fightThe catchDeck median
HP Damage it can absorb before it falls. Does nothing offensively. A wall that cannot hit still loses. 452
ATK The base of every strike it makes. The only stat that creates damage. Everything else multiplies it. 110
DEF Reduces incoming damage on a curve: 110 / (110 + DEF). Diminishing. Going from 0→110 halves damage; 110→220 only shaves a third more. 104
SPD Acts earlier, and may act twice in a round. Saturates by design — even a perfect roll buys one extra swing. 76
MOM Adds this % of its own attack every round, up to +220%. Worth nothing in round one, and nothing at all on a card with no attack. 12
The trap worth understanding

SPD and MOM do not deal damage — they multiply an attack the card already owns. Because the budget is zero-sum, spending heavily on them means having little attack left to multiply. Two swings of a feeble attack still lose to one swing of a real one. This single fact explains most of the balance work on this game.

Step four

The element ring

Element comes from what a channel is about, not how big it is — the only signal in the game that isn't a re-expression of the three numbers. Each element beats the next one round the ring and loses to the one before it. Attacking into an advantage is ×1.25; into a disadvantage, ×0.8. Unaligned neither counters nor is countered.

Gaming beats Tech — 10.7% of deck
Tech beats Knowledge — 3.6%
Knowledge beats Music — 10.6%
Music beats Comedy — 47.5%
Comedy beats Lifestyle — 10.5%
Lifestyle beats Gaming — 14.7%
Unaligned — neutral both ways — 2.3%

Music is nearly half the deck, which makes Knowledge cards quietly valuable (they beat Music) and Comedy cards quietly awkward (Music beats them). That imbalance is a property of who has a Wikipedia entry, not a design choice.

Step five

One attack, term by term

Every strike in the game is a single product. Attack is the only term that creates damage; the rest scale it up or down.

BaseATK The attacker's attack stat.
× Ramp1 → 3.2 Momentum, compounding each round. Doubled for a Riser, capped at +220%.
× Mitigation110/(110+DEF) The defender's armour. At DEF 110 it halves the hit.
× Element0.8 / 1 / 1.25 Ring matchup. Unaligned is always 1.
× Situation0.85 / 0.75 / 1.6 Reaching from the back rank, Aegis cover, or a Carry executing.
× Swing0.75 – 1.25 The luck dial. ±25% on every single hit.
× Crit1.6 On a roll under the card's crit chance — 5% to 45%.
Where crit comes from

Crit is mostly punch, a little speed: 0.05 + 0.95 × punch share + 0.20 × speed share, capped at 45%. A critical hit means a video that landed far above this channel's normal — so it belongs on views-per-upload, not on how often someone posts. A small channel whose uploads overperform its subscriber count is the most dangerous thing in the game per point of budget.

Step six

How a round runs

  1. Roll extra actions. Every living unit gets one turn; fast ones may earn a second, rolled once at the top of the round.
  2. Sort. Fastest first. All second actions come after all first actions — "everyone acts, then the quick ones act again" is something a player can follow while watching.
  3. Each unit picks a target. An Assassin ignores the wall and takes the enemy's highest-attack card in the back. Otherwise only the front rank is reachable while any of it stands, and a living Titan in that rank absorbs the hit.
  4. Damage resolves by the product above, minimum 1.
  5. Repeat until one side is wiped, or 30 rounds pass. Real fights end in about six.

Two rules that live at the team level

RuleEffectWhy it exists
Rows
2 front · 3 back		 The back rank is unreachable until the front falls, but attacks from the back cost 15%. Placement becomes a real decision: safety is paid for in damage.
Formation bonus
4 classes +2.5% · 5 classes +5%		 Applied to every stat on every unit in the team. It is the one advantage a per-card rating cannot see — which is exactly why Auto-pick can't find it and you can.

That bonus is larger than it looks. A lift on every stat raises both health and damage, so a nominal +5% is closer to +10% of real fighting strength.

Bad Bunny out-rates MrBeast with a tenth of the subscribers. His 190 videos average 257M views each against MrBeast's 137M — so his punch residual is far higher, which buys attack and, now, crit. Size bought MrBeast a marginally bigger budget and a decade of age bought him health; neither is worth as much as landing twice as hard per upload.

PewDiePie is the clearest illustration of the whole design. 110M subscribers and one of the largest view counts on the platform — but spread across 4,665 videos, which is 6.3M per upload against what a 110M-subscriber channel is expected to manage. His punch residual sits on the floor. He is old, so he is tough: HP 641, the highest of the three. He is prolific, so he is fast: SPD 160. And he hits for 28. That is not a bug — it is the model saying, accurately, that this is a channel whose recent uploads underperform its own audience.
